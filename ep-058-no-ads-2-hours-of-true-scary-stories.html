It is a big, limestone building from the late eighteen hundreds with high ceilings that hold the heat way up at the top. They didn't talk much, and when they did, it was in a low murmur that sounded like dry leaves blowing across pavement.

The hall smelled like old cedar and unwashed wool, mixed with the faint scent of the peppermint tea they kept brewing in the kitchenette. Around ten o'clock, I walked my rounds, checking the locks on the heavy oak doors and making sure the furnace wasn't rattling too loudly. Everything felt solid and quiet, just the way a night shift should feel when you're alone.

I remember noticing the grandfather clock in the foyer because the wood was so dark it almost looked purple in the shadows. It was a massive piece of furniture, easily seven feet tall, with a brass pendulum that swung back and forth with a heavy, metallic thud. It kept perfect time, marking every fifteen minutes with a chime that echoed all the way up to the rafters.

Around midnight, the leader of the guild, a woman named Martha, came over to my desk. She was wearing a thick gray cardigan and holding a plastic cup of water, her movements very slow and deliberate.
