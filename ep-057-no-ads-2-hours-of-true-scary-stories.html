I went back to the desk and started my paperwork. I kept the desk lamp low. The clock on the wall ticked every second, very loudly.

I didn't look up when the sun started to rise. I could see the light changing in the hallway behind me. The shadows of the chairs grew longer and thinner.

When the day shift nurse arrived, she looked tired. She asked me if anything happened during the night. I told her it was a quiet shift.

I walked past that window one last time on my way out. The sun was fully up now, bright and yellow. The ice was still there, thick and white against the glass.

She didn't seem to notice it as she walked by with her coffee. I didn't point it out to her. I just put my coat on and left the building.

The air outside was eighty degrees and humid. I walked to my car and sat in the driver's seat. I turned the heater on, even though I was sweating.

I drove home and checked my own windows. Everything looked normal. I laid down in bed and watched the curtains move in the breeze.

I didn't sleep much that day. I kept thinking about how the sun didn't melt it. It is still there, I think.

The end
