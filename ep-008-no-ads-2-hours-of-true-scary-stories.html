The High Lodge is a quiet place in the late fall, just before the first heavy snows bring the guests up the mountain. I was there alone for a week, getting the rooms ready and checking the inventory for the opening weekend. It is all heavy cedar beams and thick wool rugs that smell like lanolin and wood smoke.

Barkley was with me, a big mountain dog with fur like a dense rug who had been the resort mascot for six years. He usually spent his days sleeping by the main hearth, but since we were the only ones there, he followed me everywhere. The sound of his claws clicking on the polished floorboards was the only rhythm in the building.

My routine was simple and I liked it that way. I would start in the basement laundry, move up to the dining hall, and then work my way through the guest wings one floor at a time. I carried a clipboard and a heavy ring of keys that jangled against my hip as I walked.

The air inside was always still, but you could feel the cold trying to push its way through the glass. I spent most of Tuesday checking the fireplaces in the south wing to make sure the dampers were working right. Barkley stayed right at my heels, occasionally nudging my hand with his cold nose while I worked.

By Wednesday afternoon, I had reached the third floor. This is where the lodge narrows out, with a long central hallway that splits into the north and south wings at a wide, carpeted landing. The sun was starting to dip behind the peaks, turning everything inside a sort of dusty orange color.

I finished the south wing and walked back toward the landing to start on the north side. Barkley was trotting ahead of me, his tail wagging low and slow like it always did when he was content. I remember thinking about what I was going to make for dinner that night, maybe some soup and toast.

As we reached the center of the landing, Barkley stopped. He didn't slow down or sniff around first; he just came to a complete halt right where the blue carpet of the landing meets the dark oak flooring of the north wing. It was so sudden that I almost tripped over him.

I called his name and kept walking, expecting him to follow me into the north hallway. I got about ten feet in before I realized the clicking sound of his paws had stopped entirely. I turned around and saw him sitting perfectly upright on the blue carpet, his chest broad and his ears forward.

He wasn't growling or acting scared, which is why it felt so strange at the time. He was just looking past me, staring into the empty air of the north wing with a kind of intense focus. I walked back to him and patted his head, but he didn't even blink or look at me.

I thought maybe he smelled a mouse or a squirrel that had gotten into the walls over the summer. I reached into my pocket and pulled out his favorite toy, a piece of cedar wood he liked to chew on. I tossed it a few feet into the north wing, and it landed with a dull thud on the wood floor.

Barkley loves to fetch, but he didn't move an inch to go get it. He just watched the toy slide across the floor and then brought his eyes back up to the empty space in the middle of the hallway. I know how dogs are, but this didn't feel like a dog seeing a ghost; it felt like he was respecting a boundary.

I walked over, picked up the toy, and tried to lead him by his collar. He didn't resist exactly, but he braced his weight against the floor and refused to let his front paws cross that seam where the wood started. I didn't want to hurt him, so I let go and stood there for a minute.

The north wing was perfectly clean and the air was the same temperature as the rest of the floor. There were no drafts, no weird smells, and the lights were all working fine when I flicked the switch. I walked all the way to the end of the hall and back, just to show him it was okay.

Every time I looked back, he was still there, sitting exactly on the edge of the carpet. He looked like a statue, watching me move through the rooms. When I finally came back to the landing, he stood up, wagged his tail, and followed me down to the kitchen like nothing had happened.

I sat in the kitchen that night and tried to tell myself it was just a weird animal quirk. Maybe there was a cleaning chemical used on the north wing floors that he didn't like the smell of. But I've worked here a long time, and we use the same supplies for the whole building.

The lodge gets very quiet at night, and you start to notice every little sound the wood makes as it settles. I kept looking toward the stairs, thinking about that empty hallway upstairs. I knew the rooms were all locked and the windows were shut tight, just like I left them.

Barkley was lying at my feet, sleeping soundly and twitching his paws like he was chasing something in a dream. He seemed totally fine now that we were on the ground floor. I tried to focus on my book, but I kept thinking about that cedar toy sitting on the floor upstairs.

I eventually finished my tea and turned off the kitchen lights to go to bed. I walked past the stairs and noticed that Barkley stayed close to my leg, keeping his head down. The north wing was up there in the dark, perfectly still, and for some reason, I decided I wouldn't go back up there until morning.

The lodge is perfectly clean, and the fires are always burning, and I just live with the rest of it.
