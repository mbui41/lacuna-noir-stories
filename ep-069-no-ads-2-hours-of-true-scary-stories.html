I took the job because I needed the silence. It was a security gig for a business park they had just finished building on the edge of the county, about twenty minutes past the last gas station. The pay was decent enough for someone whose only job was to stay awake.

The farm gets very quiet in the weeks between the harvest and the first hard freeze. The fields are all turned over, and the air smells like damp soil and cold metal. It is the kind of quiet that feels heavy, like the land is holding its breath.

I grew up here, and I am used to the way the wind sounds when it moves through the empty corn stalks left in the southern patches. But the north field is different this year. It is a wide, flat expanse of dirt that looks like a gray ocean under the clouds.

My routine is always the same during this season. I check the locks on the barn, turn off the mudroom light, and go to bed around ten. I live alone now, and the house is far too large for just one person, but I usually sleep well.
