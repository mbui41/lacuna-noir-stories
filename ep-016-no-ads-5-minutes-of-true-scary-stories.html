Story 1 of 1The Third Cup

I used to work in a municipal records office on the third floor of a brick building. It was a temporary job, filing old property taxes from the seventies. The office was quiet and smelled like cold paper and floor wax.

I worked the eleven to seven shift alone because the pay was a little higher. The only sound was the hum of the fluorescent lights and the heavy clock on the wall. It ticked louder than any clock I have ever owned.

In the break room, there was an old coffee machine that took quarters. It was one of those machines that drops a paper cup down and fills it with dark, thin coffee. It made a loud mechanical whir whenever you pressed the button.

I always took my break at two in the morning to stay awake. I would put my two quarters in, wait for the cup to fill, and then walk back to my desk. I never stayed in the break room because the air felt heavy there.

One Tuesday, I realized I had left my sugar packets on the counter. I turned around and walked back into the room just as the door was closing. The machine was whirring again, even though I hadn't touched it.

I watched as a second paper cup dropped into the slot. The machine filled it to the brim with steaming black coffee, just like it had for me. There was no one else in the building, and the machine wasn't broken.

I know how this sounds, like a simple electrical glitch or a stuck button. I just figured the machine was old and I went back to my filing. I left that second cup sitting there in the tray, cooling off.

The next night, the same thing happened at exactly two-fifteen. I took my cup, walked to the door, and heard the clatter of the machine starting up again. I didn't go back in to look that time, I just went to my desk.

On Thursday, I decided to wait inside the room to see what happened. I got my coffee and stood by the sink, watching the machine. Nothing happened for five minutes, so I finally decided to leave.

As soon as my hand touched the door handle to exit, the machine clicked. It waited until I was halfway out the door before it dropped a second cup. Then it waited until the door was almost shut to drop a third one.

I opened the door wide and looked at the three cups sitting in the tray. Mine was in my hand, and two others were steaming in the machine. They were perfectly lined up, as if someone was waiting to take them.

I stopped drinking coffee after that and started bringing a thermos from home. I still had to walk past the break room to get to the filing cabinets. Every time I passed the door, I could hear the machine cycling through.

It sounded like it was making four or five cups every time I walked by the hallway. I never went back in to check if the tray was overflowing or if the floor was wet. I just kept my eyes on the files and finished my shift.

I quit that job a week later and found something in a grocery store during the day. Sometimes when I'm in a quiet kitchen, I think I hear a paper cup drop. I don't look behind me when I leave the room anymore.

The end
