I’m not a sailor, but I can follow instructions, and the owner is a friend of mine. He needed someone to sit on the boat while he went back to town for the night to handle some business at the harbor office. It was a clear evening with almost no swell.

The cove is quiet, tucked away behind a stand of tall pines that block most of the wind. You can hear the water lapping against the hull, a soft hollow sound that usually helps people sleep. I felt safe there, just me and the creaking of the wood.

I spent the first few hours reading a book by the light of a small brass lamp bolted to the bulkhead. The smell of the cabin was mostly old teak oil and salt, which isn't unpleasant once you get used to it. Everything felt heavy and still in the way things do when you're alone.

Around ten o'clock, I made myself a cup of tea on the small gimbaled stove. I remember watching the blue flame flicker and thinking about how far away the town felt, even though it was just over the ridge. The silence out there is different than the silence in a house.
